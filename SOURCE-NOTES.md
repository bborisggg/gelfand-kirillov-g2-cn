# Sources for the revised manuscript

The references below identify the input to each argument. Page numbers for journal articles refer to the printed pagination unless an accepted manuscript is specified.

## The two valuation bounds

- L. Ein, R. Lazarsfeld and K. E. Smith, *Uniform approximation of Abhyankar valuation ideals in smooth function fields*, American Journal of Mathematics 125 (2003), 409–440, Theorem 2.1, p. 420. For a nontrivial discrete valuation of a function field over a trivially valued field, the rational rank is one. The Zariski–Abhyankar inequality then bounds one plus the residue-field transcendence degree by the transcendence degree of the original field. The reverse inequality for a canonical position field comes from the manuscript's continuous derivations. [Author's copy](https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~roblaz/Reprints/Ein.Laz.Smith.Abhyankar.Vals.pdf)
- D. Eisenbud, *Commutative Algebra with a View Toward Algebraic Geometry*, GTM 150, Springer, 1995, Theorem 7.8, p. 190. In a complete Noetherian local ring containing a field with separable residue extension, one may choose a coefficient field containing the given field and prescribed lifts of a differential basis. In characteristic zero a transcendence basis serves as a differential basis. The completed valuation ring here is a discrete valuation ring, hence its fraction field is a Laurent-series field over the chosen coefficients. [Theorem 7.8](https://www.math.ens.psl.eu/~benoist/refs/Eisenbud.pdf) The formal-smoothness proof is also given in [Stacks, Tag 032A](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/032A) together with [Tag 0322](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0322).

## The four-branch surface

- J.-L. Colliot-Thélène and A. N. Skorobogatov, *The Brauer–Grothendieck Group*. In the authors’ manuscript dated 31 October 2019, Example 5.2.10 (p. 133) treats a separable norm polynomial with an even-degree factor. Proposition 6.2.1 (p. 148) identifies the kernel of the Brauer restriction for a smooth conic in characteristic different from two. For the function-field formulation use injectivity from the regular conic to its generic point, as in the example itself. Corollary 5.2.5 (p. 130) gives the stable birational invariance of the unramified Brauer group. The two factors used here both have simple rational roots, so their nonzero residue patterns give the required nonconstant class. [Authors’ 2019 text](https://www.imo.universite-paris-saclay.fr/~jean-louis.colliot-thelene/BGgroup_book.pdf)
- The 2021 Springer edition has different chapter numbers. The relevant chapters are 6, *Birational invariance*, and 7, *Severi–Brauer varieties and hypersurfaces*. The item numbers in the preceding bullet refer explicitly to the 2019 text. [Published Chapter 6](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-74248-5_6) · [Published Chapter 7](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-74248-5_7)

## The minimal algebra in type G₂

- A. Petukhov, *On the Gelfand–Kirillov conjecture for the W-algebras attached to the minimal nilpotent orbits*, Journal of Algebra 470 (2017), 289–299. Theorem 2.1 (accepted manuscript, printed p. 2) gives an isomorphism of quotient division rings after tensoring the minimal finite W-algebra with a Weyl algebra of rank one plus half the dimension of the degree-one space. Table 1 identifies the latter as the four-dimensional irreducible of sl₂ in type G₂. These results are over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. [Accepted manuscript](https://pure.manchester.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/47075822/WtoJAlgRev2PR.pdf) · [Journal issue](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2016.08.021)
- A. Premet, *Enveloping algebras of Slodowy slices and the Joseph ideal*, JEMS 9 (2007), 487–543. Theorem 6.1 (pp. 525–526) gives the generators and the bracket with the central Casimir contribution. Remark 6.5 (p. 531) treats G₂ explicitly, including the nonzero square in the highest component of the bracket. The PBW statement for the Kazhdan filtration is recalled on p. 488, citing Premet’s *Special transverse slices and their enveloping algebras*, Theorem 4.6. The identification with invariant differential operators on traceless three-by-three matrices is a further deduction in the manuscript. [JEMS text](https://eprints.maths.manchester.ac.uk/796/1/Enveloping_Algebras.pdf) · [Original PBW result](https://doi.org/10.1006/aima.2001.2063)

## The parabolic extension

- E. Backelin and K. Kremnizer, *Singular localization of g-modules and applications to representation theory*, JEMS 17 (2015), 2763–2787. Definition 4.2 (p. 2771) takes invariant differential operators on the right Levi torsor and gives the local product description. Section 4.4 (p. 2775) identifies the extended enveloping algebra fixed by the Levi Weyl group with the tensor product of the enveloping algebra and the Levi centre over the full centre. Theorem 4.10(iii) (p. 2776) identifies this algebra with the derived global sections, so its degree-zero part is precisely the required global algebra. These statements apply to the extended sheaf before fixing a central character. The passage from global sections to the division ring on the big cell uses the manuscript's denominator argument. [Definition 4.2](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/32079#page=9) · [Section 4.4](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/32079#page=13) · [Theorem 4.10](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/32079#page=14)

## The status of the conjecture

- A. Premet, *Modular Lie algebras and the Gelfand–Kirillov conjecture*, Inventiones Mathematicae 181 (2010), 395–420, Theorem 1.1. Its hypothesis is a simple Lie algebra over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. The author explicitly starts his list of excluded orthogonal types at B₃, consistent with the exceptional isomorphism B₂ ≅ C₂. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0907.2500)
- V. Futorny, J. T. Hartwig, E. C. Jauch and J. Schwarz, *Galois rings: ring-theoretic properties and applications to Coulomb branches and affine Hecke algebras*, version 3, 14 September 2026. Corollary 7.10 concerns realisation as a spherical Coulomb branch algebra. The historical status of types C and G₂ is recorded in the remark immediately following its proof. [Section 7.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.10782v3#S7.SS2)

## Shifted highest weights in type C₂

Harish-Chandra, *On some applications of the universal enveloping algebra of a semisimple Lie algebra*, Transactions of the American Mathematical Society 70 (1951), 28–96, Part III, Lemma 36 and Theorem 5. The paper uses the central-character evaluation and the Weyl orbit of the shifted highest weight. The angular Casimir normalization and the polynomial comparison on the weight grid are calculated in §4. [Primary source](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1951-0044515-0)

## Trace generators in type G₂

Aslaksen, Drensky and Sadikova, *Defining relations of invariants of two 3×3 matrices*, Theorem 1.2 and equation (9). Passing to traceless matrices removes the two linear traces. Transposition fixes the seven quadratic and cubic traces and the quartic, and changes the sign of the sextic generator. The Hilbert series in Proposition 5.1 follows by taking the fixed subalgebra. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0405389)
